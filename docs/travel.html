Travel windows

In reports from 2022 to 2025, past exchange students at KAIST say they travelled every weekend in the first weeks, then almost stopped around midterms and finals. Lectures are mostly Monday to Thursday, and my own timetable left me long weekends. In fall 2025 midterms were 20 to 24 October and finals 15 to 19 December (see Academics), and my December was crammed with projects, presentations and exams from the first week, so do your travelling early. Attendance counts in a lot of courses, so check before you stretch a weekend into a class day.

The dates change every year, so check KAIST’s academic calendar at kaist.ac.kr/en/html/edu/03100101.html before you book anything.

Jeju

Jeju is the big island off Korea’s south coast. In mid-September 2025, two weeks into the semester, about 20 of us exchange students went there for 3 to 4 days. We were a real mix, with Danes, Poles, Germans, French, a Swede, a Romanian and a Lithuanian from DTU. We stayed in the same hotel, ate together, hiked and surfed.

The big hike was Hallasan, the mountain in the middle of the island, and it rained the whole time. We got almost to the top before the park closed the mountain because of the weather, so we took our shoes off and walked barefoot on the wet rocks, which felt “like a new sense unlocked”. On the south side of the island we went bodyboarding. The waves were big but not scary, the rain was cold and the water was warm. We also visited a tea museum that sold luxury tea.

The whole trip was “like a beach episode of an anime”, and it’s one of those core memories you only know you’ll miss the day after you leave. I’d known the group for two weeks, so say yes to the big group trip even if it comes early.

Uber worked on Jeju as well. On the hike day I took five rides on the island for 54,400 KRW in total, paid to the driver in person.

The nearest airport to KAIST is Cheongju International Airport. Figures from 2024 to 2026 put it at about 20 flights a day to Jeju, taking around 1 hour 10 minutes and starting from about 29 USD (US dollars) one-way. In 2024 a bus from Yuseong, the district of Daejeon that KAIST is in, took 40 to 50 minutes and cost 6,500 KRW, but it only ran 8 times a day, so plan your flight around the bus.

Hallasan bookings

Only the two trails that reach the summit, Seongpanak and Gwaneumsa, need a booking, which you make on the park’s reservation site, visithalla.jeju.go.kr. The other trails need no booking but stop short of the top. Bookings open at 09:00 Korean time on the first working day of the month before your hike, so a September hike opens at the start of August. One booking covers up to four people, but every hiker shows their own QR code and passport at the gate, and people who don’t show up get penalised, so cancel if you can’t go (park notice, May 2025).

I booked on 7 September 2025, six days ahead, for just myself on the Seongpanak trail on 13 September, with an entry window from 05:00 to 08:00. At 11:25 that day the park emailed that climbing was “fully restricted” because of the weather, which is the closure we ran into near the top. Get to the trailhead inside your entry window, and be ready for the park to close the mountain in bad weather.

Seoul

I went to Seoul several times, including to pick up my fiancée at Incheon, Seoul’s main airport, and to fly to Rome.

If you’re collecting someone at Incheon, an Uber from there to Sinchon in western Seoul took me just over an hour and cost 62,600 KRW including the motorway toll in October 2025. For Halloween the whole Jeju group went to Seoul in costume, and it was an incredible night (see Food and nights out).

Trains and buses

For trips around Korea you have the high-speed train and the express bus. In fall 2025 the two high-speed operators, KTX and SRT (Super Rapid Train), had separate apps and left from different stations in Seoul (Seoul or Yongsan for KTX, Suseo for SRT), which confused a lot of people. They merged in September 2026 and fares dropped, so the fares below are on the high side now.

Paying with a foreign card was hit and miss in 2025. Korail, the national rail operator, let you book on its website with your passport and a foreign card, but reports from 2023 to 2025 say the extra security check from your bank (3D Secure) often failed. Intercity bus terminals have accepted foreign cards since 31 July 2024, and so do the Bustago and InterCity Bus booking apps. If a booking site won’t take your card, buy the ticket at the counter.

Book early, especially for weekends. In a 2023 report, weekend trains to Seoul sold out a week ahead.

Foreigners who have lived in Korea for more than 6 months can’t buy the Korail Pass, a tourist rail pass, and in 2025 it was unclear whether a 4-month exchange student with a residence card qualifies, so ask before you pay for one.

Chuseok break

Chuseok is Korea’s big autumn holiday, and in 2025 it combined with other public holidays into a full week off.

Date (2025)Holiday
3 OctoberNational Foundation Day
5 to 7 OctoberChuseok (main day 6 October)
8 OctoberSubstitute holiday
9 OctoberHangul Day

That made 3 to 9 October a week without classes, and skipping Friday 10 October stretched it to 10 days. The break sits just before midterms. Campus cafeterias close over Chuseok, and trains around the holiday sell out days in advance. If you want to leave Korea over the break, read the visa rules further down first.

The dates move every year. In 2026 Chuseok falls on 24 to 26 September, and National Foundation Day and Hangul Day land on a Saturday and a Friday, so there is no week-long October break.

Rome in midterm week

In October 2025 I flew to Rome for a family trip with my father’s family and my fiancée. I left on 14 October and landed back at Incheon at 16:10 on 20 October, the first day of midterm week. I booked the flights in May, Finnair via Helsinki out and T’way Air direct back, for 8,275 DKK.

I booked before I had a timetable, so the trip ended up shaping which courses I took, and I missed one midterm (see Academics). Check exam dates before you book.

Visa rules for trips abroad

The embassy says the student visa is single entry, and leaving Korea on a single-entry visa before your residence card is issued uses the visa up (see Before you fly). Get the card before any trip abroad, ideally by applying directly at the Global Talent Visa Center on campus (see Arrival and first weeks). I had my card before Rome, and getting back into Korea was very easy.

Flights around Asia

Apart from Rome I didn’t leave Korea, so this part comes from research. In a 2026 snapshot, one-way flights from Incheon started at about 54 USD to Fukuoka, 56 USD to Osaka, 80 USD to Taipei and 53 USD to Da Nang, with September the cheapest month. Cheongju Airport also flies to Japan, Taipei and Da Nang. The fast ferry from Busan to Fukuoka (Queen Beetle) stopped in December 2024, but in 2025 to 2026 listings the overnight New Camellia ferry left Busan at 22:30 and arrived at 07:30, for 12,000 JPY (Japanese yen) one-way in second class. One past exchange student left the return flight open and toured Japan, Taiwan and Hong Kong after finals.
