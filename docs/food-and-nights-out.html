Meals without a kitchen

The dorms have no kitchens, so my friends and I ate out almost every day. Food in Korea is cheap enough that “you can live for almost no money”. I spent about 100 DKK a day on food, roughly 21,500 KRW, which is on the high side because I eat a lot, and you can easily spend much less. A 2023 exchange student reported spending about 300 euros a month on food.

There’s no meal plan, and the campus cafeterias take bank cards or your student card but not cash (2024 to 2026). Almost everything on campus closes on Sundays, and the cafeterias also close over Chuseok, the Korean harvest holiday (see Travel). On those days you eat from a convenience store or off campus.

Kaimaru and the cafeterias

Kaimaru (N11) is the best food place on campus. Get a bowl of rice with meat on it. Just thinking about it, my mouth is salivating.

By May 2025 Kaimaru had been renovated, with about 900 seats and vendors like Little Hanoi, Rolling Pasta and Bear’s Taco, and it gets long queues at lunchtime. It has served a subsidised 1,000 KRW breakfast on weekdays since May 2023, though in earlier years the money ran out before the semester ended, so check whether it’s still running when you arrive.

The West cafeteria in W2 serves on weekdays from 08:00 to 09:30, 11:30 to 13:30 and 17:00 to 19:00, and its menus are online in English with allergen codes.

Other places to eat on campus, from KAIST’s 2026 listing:

  • Quiznos sandwiches (N13-1), open 08:00 to 22:00 every day
  • Pulbitmaru (N12), with halal and vegan options
  • Ohh! Salad (N7-1)
  • DDDN Pizza (W2), closed on Sundays
  • California Bakery (E6)
  • Dunkin’ (W1)

Convenience-store meals

The convenience store in the dorm basement was my backup kitchen. Lean chicken breast microwaved with rice, maybe with some canned tuna, makes a proper meal that’s fairly healthy and very cheap. KAIST listed the W6 store as closed in 2026, so you may have to use another dorm’s store (see Campus life).

Coffee and cafés

Coffee is cheap at budget chains like Mega Coffee, Compose and Paik’s, and a 2024 exchange report says the campus cafés are discounted too. On campus, Angelinus (N13-1) is open until midnight on weekdays and Caffe Pascucci (E3) from 08:00 to 23:00, and there are Tom N Toms (N1), Cafe Ogada (E9) and Dream Book Cafe (E9), which listed drinks at 1,000 to 2,800 KRW in 2026.

Korean barbecue

Korean barbecue is everywhere in Daejeon, it’s cheap, and it’s often all-you-can-eat, which made it our standard Friday night. All-you-can-eat Korean barbecue and a traditional Korean spa are, for me, “two of the most peak experiences a human can experience”.

Korean spas

A group of Kazakh friends, one of them with a car, showed us the good spas in Daejeon (see People). I don’t remember the names, so search for 찜질방 (jjimjilbang, a Korean bathhouse) in Naver Map or KakaoMap, the two Korean map apps. For the everyday version, there’s the cheap sauna on campus (see Campus life).

Finding restaurants

I don’t remember my favourite restaurants’ names either. To find good ones, search for Korean barbecue in Korean, so you get the places locals go to and skip the tourist traps. Words to try are 고기집 (gogijip, meat restaurant) and 무한리필 (muhan ripil, unlimited refills). Use Naver Map or KakaoMap, because Google Maps works badly in Korea.

The other method is to walk around and follow your nose. “As soon as you find something that smells irresistible, go in with your friends.”

If you want Daejeon’s own food, the city named bread, kalguksu (knife-cut noodle soup) and dubu-duruchigi (spicy stir-fried tofu) as its three signature dishes in April 2026. The 2025 Daejeon Bread Festival ran on 18 and 19 October on Soje-dong Cafe Street, with 102 bakeries taking part.

Sungsimdang bakery

Sungsimdang is Daejeon’s famous bakery and only exists in Daejeon. It’s known for twigim soboro (a fried streusel bun) and buchu-ppang (chive bread). The main store is near Jungang-ro subway station and is open every day from 08:00 to 22:00. The branch closest to KAIST is at the DCC (Daejeon Convention Center), and there are others at Daejeon Station and the Lotte Department Store. The DCC branch doesn’t sell twigim soboro, but a shop called Tuiso Jeongeojang across the street does. Queues get long, and in December 2025 people waited more than 5 hours for the strawberry cake (49,000 KRW), which isn’t sold online.

Delivery apps

Food delivery is harder to set up as a foreigner. Students between 2023 and 2026 reported that the delivery apps are unreliable until you have your residence card and that Coupang Eats may refuse foreign cards, and sources disagree on whether Baemin takes them. Shuttle Delivery is an English-language delivery app that takes international cards.

Vegetarian and allergy tips

Vegetarians and people with allergies have a harder time. Anchovy or beef stock and jeotgal (salted seafood, which is in most kimchi) hide in many dishes, and asking for no meat often still gets you seafood. Say no meat, no fish, no seafood and no fish sauce. Pulbitmaru (N12) on campus has had vegan and halal options, and HappyCow, a vegetarian restaurant app, lists places in Daejeon.

Bars near campus

I’d send you to Santa Claus, a bar about 15 to 20 minutes on foot from the West Gate, the campus exit nearest W6. I think it’s in Gung-dong, the neighbourhood around Chungnam National University just outside the gate, which KAIST’s page about the area describes as the place for cheap Korean restaurants and the student bars closest to campus.

Going-out areas

Online guides name three nightlife areas in Daejeon. Besides Gung-dong, there’s Dunsan, with Galleria Timeworld and other department stores, international food and bigger bars, and Eunhaeng-dong, the old downtown. Beyond the bars, Daejeon has good shopping streets, bakeries, arcades and museums.

KAIST’s free shuttle passes Chungnam National University, Wolpyeong Station and Galleria Timeworld, but according to KAIST’s undated page it only runs 9 times on weekdays, between 09:05 and 18:05, so late nights mean a taxi home (see Arrival and first weeks for fares and the night surcharge).

Drinking etiquette

A 2023 exchange report and general guides list a few rules for drinking with Koreans, especially older ones:

  • Never pour your own drink.
  • Pour and receive with two hands for anyone older, and turn slightly away when you drink in front of them.
  • Always order food with your drinks.

Halloween in Seoul

For the biggest nights out, we went to Seoul (see Travel), and Halloween 2025 there was an incredible night. The group of exchange students I’d been to Jeju Island with went dressed as anime characters, and my fiancée, who was visiting, went with me as Link and Zelda from the Zelda video games. We did four clubs in Hongdae, the area around Hongik University, which felt like “the most youthful part of Seoul”. Koreans kept asking us for photos, and their own costumes were great.
