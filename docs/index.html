KAIST, the short version

A quick guide to an exchange semester at KAIST (Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology) in Daejeon, South Korea, for students at DTU (Technical University of Denmark). Everything here comes from my own fall 2025 semester.

By Elias, BSc in Artificial Intelligence and Data at DTU.

If you only read this

  1. Land two days before orientation. I landed on the day itself, jet-lagged, and missed the welcome events.
  2. Put the course registration lottery in your calendar the day you're accepted (12 August in 2025). I forgot it and only got the leftover courses.
  3. Do all the forms on your first day, and bring Korean cash for them.
  4. Apply for your residence card directly at the immigration office on campus. Mine took 2 to 3 weeks, against about a month through the volunteer queue.
  5. Don't leave Korea before your residence card arrives. The student visa is single entry.
  6. Get a proper Korean SIM with 30 to 50 GB and calls. Living on eSIMs was a pain.
  7. Bring a backup card like Revolut or Lunar. My Danish card was refused at most cash machines.
  8. Get a bike. Class was 10 minutes' walk from my dorm, or 2 by bike.
  9. Apply for every grant you find. I sent about 20 and got 4, worth 37,000 kr.
  10. Book the airport bus home in person, a day or two ahead. I didn't, and paid 213,100 won (about 1,000 kr) for a taxi.

Should you go?

Yes, if you want a real challenge. My scores, out of 10:

  • Overall 10
  • Academics 8
  • Social life 10
  • Dorm 7
  • Food 10
  • Admin 2
  • Value 10
  • Safety 10

Go if you like team projects, want courses you can't take at DTU, and can live with paperwork. Pick somewhere else if you want a chill semester or hate exams that test memory.

Applying

  • DTU picks by GPA (grade point average) alone. The motivational letter only breaks exact ties. As I remember it, my year's cut-off for KAIST was about 9 to 9.5; DTU's 2026 page says 10.0. Rank two or three backups you'd be happy with.
  • Check whether you need an English test at all. I took the Duolingo test to be safe.
  • For the pre-approval, pick plausible courses from KAIST's catalogue. An AI tool can scan it for you, but check its picks. You can re-file as often as you need.
  • A KAIST course is 3 credits, about 5.6 ECTS (European Credit Transfer System points) for a bachelor student. My five courses came to 27.5 ECTS.
  • Bachelor students can't take graduate courses, and half your courses must come from your host department.

Money

  • No tuition. You keep your SU (the Danish state study grant), which was 6,084 kr a month after tax for me in 2025.
  • My four grants: Erik Sommerfeldt (10,000 kr, taxed), Fabrikant Vilhelm Pedersen (7,000 kr), Otto Mønsted (10,000 kr) and Vera og Carl Johan Michaelsen (10,000 kr). Write your own drafts. An AI tool can polish them, but generic letters get turned down.
  • Rough costs: return flights about 6,000 kr, the dorm about 4,000 kr for the whole semester, food about 100 kr a day (I eat a lot), and Korean health insurance 76,390 won a month, which I paid in cash at convenience stores.
  • I convert at about 215 won to 1 krone (2025), so 10,000 won is about 47 kr.

Before you fly

  • Visa. Book an appointment at the Korean embassy in Hellerup (on g4k.go.kr) as soon as your Certificate of Admission arrives. It cost 420 kr on the embassy's 2026 page, and mine came 16 days after the appointment.
  • Health form. Proof of two MMR (measles, mumps, rubella) vaccinations plus a tuberculosis blood test, about 600 kr. Due late July.
  • Insurance. DTU requires it; I had Tryg through IDA. Korean health insurance is mandatory on top.
  • Photos. The photo booth at Nørreport, about 50 kr.
  • Flights. I flew Turkish Airlines via Istanbul. The "flexible dates" add-on turned out to be useless when I had to move both legs.
  • Packing. Pack light. You'll buy clothes there, and the dorm has laundry.

Arrival

  • Incheon airport to KAIST takes about three hours: the airport bus to Daejeon (27,700 won in 2025), then a taxi. Install Uber or Kakao T before you land.
  • The first days are paperwork: the registration packet, the orientation fee and bedding (20,000 won each, in cash), the dorm fee in won, and the residence card.
  • The residence card was "one of the biggest messes ever". Skip the volunteer hall: in the International Center (building W2-1), go right past the café and up the stairs to the Global Talent Visa Center.

Courses

My tier list:

  • SCS454 AI-Based Software Engineering Evolving code with evolutionary algorithms. A whole new tool in your toolbox.
  • SCS473 Introduction to Social Computing You build your own social app. Code mixed with sociology and internet culture.
  • BCS492(C) Diffusion and Flow Models The maths and code behind image generators.
  • CMethods in Brain and Cognitive Sciences Niche and memory-heavy, but more interesting than I expected.
  • FCS360 Introduction to Database Online with your webcam on, and SQL written on paper. Take databases at DTU instead.
  • Lectures are 75 minutes, with more self-study than at DTU and no exercise sessions. Attendance counts toward your grade.
  • Exams test memory, but grading is on a curve, so it's hard to fail and hard to stand out. Grades come back to DTU as just "passed".
  • Be polite to professors and TAs (teaching assistants). They're flexible when you ask nicely.

Campus life

  • Most exchange students live in W6 (Mir and Narae Hall): double rooms, split by gender, no kitchen.
  • Eat at Kaimaru (get the rice bowl with meat) and the convenience stores. Korean barbecue and a traditional Korean spa are peak experiences, and there's a cheap sauna on campus next to the pool.
  • The campus is big, green and beautiful. Get a bike.

People and travel

  • We were about 200 exchange students. The buddy programme is hit or miss, so say yes, then find your own people. Everyone is as nervous as you in the first days.
  • Koreans are friendly when you approach them but rarely start the conversation.
  • Go out in Gung-dong, near the West Gate, and save the big nights for Seoul. Halloween in Hongdae was incredible.
  • Travel early, before the workload hits. A Jeju trip with about 20 exchange students was one of my best memories.

Leaving

  • File the Campus Clearance Form, return the bedding and do the move-out checklist, or KAIST holds back your transcript.
  • The dorm deposit only goes back to a Korean bank account. I didn't have one and used a friend's.
  • Book the airport bus in person at the Daejeon Government Complex stop a day or two ahead, and leave in the morning. Buses sell out around Christmas.
  • My transcript arrived by email in mid-January, and DTU transferred the credits after that.
