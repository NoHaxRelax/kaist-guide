꿀팁Honey tip: Apply for every grant, the small ones included, since they’re the ones you’re most likely to get.

Exchange students pay nothing to KAIST, and DTU charges Danish and other EU (European Union) students no tuition either, so the exchange costs no tuition at all. Your money goes on flights, rent at home if you keep a place, and daily life in Daejeon, which is cheap enough that “you can live for almost no money”, depending on how you spend it. I gave KAIST 10 out of 10 for value for money.

SU abroad

You keep your SU during an exchange, as long as the stay gives you full credit toward your degree. My 2025 notice gave me 7,086 DKK a month before tax and 6,084 DKK after, though your tax depends on your own situation. I didn’t take the SU loan, but you can keep that abroad too. For 2026 a third-party source lists the grant at 7,426 DKK and the loan at 3,799 DKK a month, and su.dk has the current rates.

If you’re registered at your parents’ address, report the stay in minSU, SU’s self-service site (under Ret din SU and then Oplys særlige bopælsforhold), before you go. That gets you the higher rate for students living away from home, and it only counts from the month you report it, never backwards.

My first plan also counted on the udlandsstipendium, SU’s grant for studying abroad, and my notes had it down for 28,500 DKK. It never came, because it doesn’t apply to exchange stays. As I understand it, it exists to pay tuition fees abroad, and an exchange has none. There’s no Erasmus money for Korea either, since DTU’s Erasmus+ grants only cover exchanges inside the EU and the EEA (European Economic Area).

The Otto Mønsted grant

Otto Mønsteds Fond gives up to 10,000 DKK to DTU engineering students, bachelor or master, and over 900 students got it in 2022, so it’s well worth applying. Applications are open from 10 January to 1 March each year at omf.onlinelegat.dk, and answers come in May. My 2025 grant came with these conditions.

  • a GPA (grade point average) of at least 8
  • a credit-giving stay of at least 3 months, with at least 15 approved ECTS points
  • payment about two months after you upload DTU’s official pre-approval of your courses, which they wanted by October or November 2025
  • a report of half a page to a page, uploaded within six months after the stay ends
  • photos for their Instagram, which was optional

Because of the payment rule, the money comes during or after the semester, so don’t count on it for the flights. Check omfonden.dk for the current rules.

Grant applications

The rule in my planning notes was “Søg alt! Mindst 50, måske 100. Ikke forsøm de små legater, de er der større sandsynlighed for at få.” In English, apply for everything, at least 50 and maybe 100, and don’t skip the small grants, because they’re the ones you’re most likely to get. I only got to about 20, and even the smallest of my four grants covered my return flights on its own.

The deadlines are spread over a whole year, so start early. I sent my first application in August 2024, before I’d even applied to DTU for the exchange, most of the others in two long sittings in January 2025, and a last few in May. Put every deadline in a spreadsheet or a calendar. Four funds in my tracker are marked “glemte at søge bruh” (“forgot to apply, bruh”), namely Ørsteds Studielegater, Olsens Mindefond, Hedeselskabets Grønne Fremtid and Jørgen Buch’s Fond.

A student who had been on exchange at KAIST the year before sent me her whole pack, with a budget sheet, a sheet of what she actually spent, one of her grant applications and her list of grants. Ask around for one, because seeing a letter that worked saves you hours.

Most funds want the same attachments, so I kept these in one folder.

  • your DTU transcript in English
  • DTU’s letter confirming your exchange spot
  • your SU notice
  • your latest tax assessment (årsopgørelse)
  • a budget (mine is below)
  • sometimes your high school diploma or a letter of recommendation

Many funds use online portals such as Grant Compass. Some only put the decision inside the portal, with an email that just says your application has been processed, so log in and read it. Others only reply if you win, so silence usually means no.

Grant letters and AI

I used AI (artificial intelligence) tools on my grant letters, because writing 40 letters by hand is about a month of part-time work. Write the draft yourself, let the AI sharpen it, and then go back through it so it’s in your own words and tailored to the fund you’re writing to. Letters that read as generic, off-topic or machine-written get flagged, and AI detectors exist now, so be more careful than people had to be a couple of years ago.

Some funds didn’t fit a bachelor student in 2024 to 2025.

  • William Demant Fonden needs a finished bachelor’s degree, so it’s one for master’s students
  • Per og Lise Aarsleffs Fond was only for construction students
  • Uniclub Study Abroad didn’t work with exchanges through Beyond Borders
  • the Ramboll Scholarship was temporarily closed
  • Nordea-fonden stopped its study-abroad grants after 2023

Knud Højgaards Fond gives study travel grants for credit-giving stays of more than 3 months and has opened to bachelor students. Your application, with your pre-approval and admission letter, has to arrive before you leave. You need under 300,000 DKK in liquid assets, you only get one grant per degree, and processing takes about two months. The fund paused applications in August 2026 for a system change, so check its page before you plan around it.

DTU lists open grants on Beyond Borders under “Grant Applications” and on its scholarship page, and points to Legatbogen and Legatnet, two Danish grant search sites.

Bank cards

My card from Lån & Spar, my Danish bank, was refused at most cash machines, on campus and off, because the bank flagged the withdrawals as fraud. The bank’s support line was closed during the Korean daytime, and calling Denmark from Korea was expensive. Bring a second card, for example from Revolut or Lunar (two app-based banks), so you always carry more than one.

Many of the first-week payments in 2025 were cash only, so arrive with some won (see Arrival and first weeks). I never opened a Korean bank account, which you need to get your dorm deposit back, but I got mine anyway (see Leaving).

Korean health insurance

NHI is compulsory for student-visa holders, and you’re enrolled automatically once you’re registered as a resident (see Before you fly). In 2025 it cost 76,390 won a month (about 355 DKK) with the 50% student discount, or roughly 300,000 won over a fall semester. Without the discount it was 152,790 won a month. The discount applied if your yearly income was at most 3.6 million won and your property tax at most 135 million won.

The bills come by post to your dorm unless you switch them to email. I paid every month in cash, taking out won at a cash machine and paying at the counter of a staffed convenience store. 7-Eleven and most others take the payment, and if one doesn’t, the staff will point you to one nearby. In 2025 ISSS suggested calling the NHI English line (033-811-2000) to set up automatic payment instead.

Unpaid premiums can block visa matters, and you have to settle the last bill and end the membership before you leave (see Leaving). The current premium is on NHI’s English site.
