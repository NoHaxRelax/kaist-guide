## Before you fly

Most of the preparation lands in June and July, once KAIST has accepted you, and several steps depend on each other. All my dates are in the full timeline (see Should you go?). When ISSS sends its big instruction email (11 July in 2025), read it slowly and put every date from it in your calendar.

### Student visa

You need a student visa before you enter Korea. Nominated exchange students get the D-2-6, the exchange-student type in Korea's D-2 student category. Danes can visit Korea for 90 days without a visa, but that doesn't cover studying. KAIST was very clear that fixing the visa after you arrive takes a letter from DTU's president, the exchange agreement between the two universities, and a fine that grows every day. ISSS told us about a student who was fined over 500 euros for switching visas one week into the semester.

You apply in person at the Korean embassy in Hellerup, just north of Copenhagen. According to the embassy's page (2025 to 2026):

- the consular section is open 09:00 to 12:00 on weekdays
- since 2 January 2025 every visit needs a booking at [g4k.go.kr](https://www.g4k.go.kr), under "Reservation to visit a diplomatic mission"
- each slot covers one application, and they won't move you to an earlier slot by email
- everything is on paper, with no postal applications and no express service

Book a slot as soon as KAIST's Certificate of Admission arrives. Mine came on 23 June 2025, I booked four days later, and my appointment was on 15 July. Make sure your passport is valid and that you have no issues with South Korea.

KAIST's emails list the documents, and so does the embassy's page (2025 to 2026):

<!-- checklist:visa-documents -->
- your passport, valid for at least 6 more months, plus a copy of the photo page
- the signed visa application form
- one 3.5 × 4.5 cm photo on photo paper (printouts on ordinary paper aren't accepted)
- your original yellow health card (sundhedskort) plus a copy
- KAIST's Certificate of Admission, signed by you
- KAIST's Certificate of Business Registration, issued within the last 3 months (KAIST sent it with the admission certificate)

The embassy also asks for a Danish bank balance certificate less than 2 weeks old, unless the host university is IEQAS (International Education Quality Assurance System) certified. KAIST was certified in 2025, so you shouldn't need one, but check that it's still on the list the [embassy's visa page](https://overseas.mofa.go.kr/dk-en/wpge/m_7042/contents.do) links to, and read that page again before your appointment, since the requirements change.

The page says they keep your passport while they process the visa. They didn't keep mine, and I had brought a printed copy. The visa came by email on 31 July 2025 as a Visa Grant Notice, 16 days after the appointment. Print it and carry it with your passport. The notice lists a "Validity Period of Visa", which is the deadline for entering Korea, and a "Period of Stay", which counts from the day you enter.

I remember paying around 600 DKK, but I'm honestly not sure. The embassy's page in 2026 lists 420 DKK for a single-entry visa for stays over 90 days, which a fall semester is, paid by MobilePay and non-refundable, so go by the page. It lists no fee for a multiple-entry student visa.

### The single-entry rule

The embassy's page in 2026 says the student visa is single entry and that you can't get a multiple-entry one, and ISSS told us the same in 2025. I had a vague memory of mine being multiple entry, so trust the embassy over me here.

Anyone staying more than 90 days has to get a residence card within 90 days of arriving, and it takes at least 2 to 3 weeks to be issued (see Arrival and first weeks). If you leave Korea on a single-entry visa before the card is issued, the visa is used up, and getting back in takes a new admission certificate and a new visa. Once you have the card, you can leave and come back within a year without any extra permit. If you really must travel before the card arrives, ISSS's advice in 2025 was to get a Confirmation of Residence Permit Application and a Certificate of Alien Registration first, and to visit their office.

In 2025 Chuseok (the Korean harvest festival) and two other public holidays gave a week off from 3 to 9 October, a popular time for trips abroad, so plan any trip for after your card arrives (see Travel). I had mine before my Rome trip on 14 October because I applied directly at the immigration office on campus.

### Passport photos

You need 3.5 × 4.5 cm photos for the embassy (on photo paper) and for your KAIST student card, which ISSS wanted as an image file by 25 July 2025. I got mine from the photo booth at Nørreport station in Copenhagen for about 50 DKK. The residence card needs one more photo of the same size, and I think mine came from a photographer near the West Gate (see Arrival and first weeks).

### KAIST health form

Every new KAIST student sends in a Certificate of Health Check-up Form before arriving. In 2025 it needed two things.

- proof of two MMR (measles, mumps and rubella) doses, which for me were my childhood vaccinations
- a TB (tuberculosis) test, either an IGRA (interferon-gamma release assay) blood test or a chest X-ray

The deadline was 25 July 2025, by email to ISSS, and the same deadline covered your student card photo and an online mental-health questionnaire. I had the blood test at Danske Lægers Vaccinations Service in Søborg on 2 July, it came back negative, and the clinic filled in and signed the whole form on 7 July. The test cost about 600 DKK. Don't take it too early, since KAIST's fact sheet for 2027 wants it dated within 3 months of moving into the dorm.

The KAIST Clinic reviews every form, and students with a contagious disease such as TB get no dorm room. In 2025 the review took about three weeks, and room assignments appeared on KDS around 18 August (see Campus life). Denmark isn't on Korea's list of countries with a high TB burden, so the visa itself didn't need a chest X-ray from a designated hospital.

### Insurance

DTU requires health, liability and accident insurance for your whole stay, but doesn't insure you abroad itself, and the Danish yellow and blue health cards give no cover in Korea. DTU points to travel insurance through PF (Polyteknisk Forening, the DTU student union) or studenterforsikring.dk, and its [insurance page](https://student.dtu.dk/en/rules/practical-information/insurance-for-students) has the current advice. I had travel insurance from Tryg, a Danish insurer, through IDA (Ingeniørforeningen, the Danish engineers' union).

Korean health insurance comes on top. Once you're registered as a resident, you're automatically enrolled in NHI, which has been compulsory for student-visa holders since March 2021. Older guides, including a 2017 wiki written by DTU students, still say it's optional. NHI cover only starts once you have your foreign resident registration number, so your own insurance has to carry you through the first weeks. Money has the monthly cost and how I paid it.

You can opt out of NHI if you have private insurance, but it's hard. Under the rules ISSS sent in October 2025, you apply after your residence card is issued, with an application form, your original policy translated into Korean (ISSS won't translate it) and your residence card or passport. The policy must cover at least 70% of medical costs (or over 1 billion won), cover illness, injury, medication and, for women, maternity, and have no limit on claims or on the period. The most common reason for a rejection is a clause that excludes pre-existing conditions. Reports from other exchange students describe opt-outs as rare and often refused. I didn't try, and paid NHI every month.

### Flights

I flew Turkish Airlines via Istanbul, booked on 16 April 2025 through Travellink, an online travel agency, for 5,967 DKK return, in economy with one 23 kg checked bag. The trip out took about 15 hours including a two-hour change in Istanbul, and the trip home about 17.5 hours. I'd book at least two months ahead, since prices rise after that.

Plan to land a couple of days before orientation. I landed on the morning of orientation day and regretted it (see Arrival and first weeks).

Book the flight home after finals. Finals week in 2025 was 15 to 19 December, and exact exam times come out late (see Academics). My flight home left just after midnight on 23 December.

### The flexible-dates add-on

I paid 219 DKK extra for Travellink's "flexible travel dates" add-on. In August a conference in Nyborg came up the day after my flight (see Arrival and first weeks), so I asked to move the trip three days later.

The add-on turned out to be useless. Travellink wouldn't apply it, because both the outbound and the return leg had to change. They rebooked me anyway, for 6,284 DKK, 317 DKK more than the original, and their terms also list a 395 DKK service fee for changes plus whatever the airline charges. Before you pay for a flex add-on, read exactly what it covers, especially whether it still works when both legs move.

Another exchange student's report recommends Turkish Airlines' own student fare, which came with 40 kg of luggage and a free date change. I didn't use it myself, so check the current terms.

### Packing

I brought too many clothes that I never wore, so pack light. Shopping in Korea, and in Daejeon especially, is great, and I replaced my whole wardrobe there. The dorm has laundry rooms, and you'll want the suitcase space for the cool things you buy. You don't need bedding either, since the dorm rents out a set (see Campus life).

Install Uber on your phone before you leave, and see Arrival and first weeks for the other apps and a Korean phone plan.

### Plugs

Korea uses type C and F sockets at 220 V and 60 Hz. Danish plugs of type C and F, the round two-pin kind, fit straight in, but Danish type K plugs, the ones with the extra earth pin, don't, so bring one adapter for those. Phone and laptop chargers handle 100 to 240 V, so they work fine.

### Weather

You arrive at the hot, wet end of summer and leave in early winter. These are Daejeon's average temperatures (1991 to 2020):

| Month | Average high | Average low |
|---|---|---|
| August | 30.3 °C | 22.5 °C |
| September | 26.3 °C | 17.0 °C |
| October | 20.8 °C | 9.4 °C |
| November | 13.5 °C | 2.8 °C |
| December | 6.2 °C | -3.4 °C |

August also gets about 300 mm of rain, and during finals week the nights drop below freezing. Bring a few summer clothes and one warm layer, and buy the rest in Korea.
