## Arrival and first weeks

My first days at KAIST were stressful, and almost all of that stress was paperwork. Landing a couple of days early and doing the forms on day one takes away most of it, and I managed neither.

### Arrival day

My plan was to land a few days early and be rested for the welcome events. Then my team ended up among the winners of DM i AI (the Danish national championship in artificial intelligence), who were presented at a conference in Nyborg on 26 and 27 August 2025, so I moved my flight three days later.

I landed at Incheon, Seoul's main international airport, at 08:40 on Friday 29 August, after about 15 hours of travel from Copenhagen. Orientation started at 13:30 that afternoon and the buddy ceremony at 16:00, which is where you meet your group of Korean student buddies (see People). I made it, badly jet-lagged and having already missed the welcome concert for new international students on 26 August, and I was much less ready for the social side of the first days than I wanted to be. The paperwork deadlines start straight away too, so I had about three days to sort everything out.

Move-in opened on 21 August in 2025, more than a week before orientation. If I did it again, I'd land about two days before orientation.

### Incheon to KAIST

Plan on about three hours from the airport to your dorm, whichever way you go. The simplest route is the airport limousine bus to Daejeon, which has no stop at KAIST, so you finish the trip by taxi.

| Airport bus | Details (2025) |
|---|---|
| Where to board | Terminal 1, first floor, stop 11 (ticket booths near exits 4, 6, 8 and 10), or Terminal 2, in the Transportation Center on the basement level, stop 5 |
| Stops in Daejeon | North Daejeon interchange, Doryong-dong, Daejeon Government Complex and Daejeon Complex Terminal, in that order |
| Times | First bus 06:20 from Terminal 1 (06:00 from Terminal 2), last bus 23:20 (23:00), every 10 to 30 minutes |
| Fares from January 2025 | 27,700 KRW standard, 35,900 KRW premium, 33,200 KRW late-night, 43,100 KRW late-night premium. KAIST's own pages still list the old 23,100 to 23,700 KRW |
| Ride | 2 hours 45 minutes to over 3 hours, with no toilet stop |

[KAIST's directions page](https://io.kaist.ac.kr/about/getting.do) says to get off at Daejeon Government Complex and take a taxi from there, which costs about 5,000 KRW and takes 5 to 10 minutes. An exchange student from 2024 to 2025 recommends Doryong-dong instead, 10 to 20 minutes by taxi to the dorms. I took the airport bus and an Uber for the last bit.

By train it's AREX (Airport Railroad Express) to Seoul Station, about 50 minutes and 13,000 KRW, then the KTX to Daejeon, about an hour and 23,700 KRW, and a taxi to campus for about 8,000 KRW (2025 prices). KAIST's maths department says this costs more than the bus without saving time, and my buddy warned me that trains to Daejeon sell out, so book ahead. Train booking and fares changed in 2026 (see Travel).

### Taxis

"Make sure you have Uber when you arrive. That makes everything 100 times easier." In Korea Uber books ordinary taxis, and Kakao T, the Korean taxi app most people use, does the same job. My rides around Daejeon in 2025 usually cost 4,500 to 15,200 KRW, and a few had a booking fee of 3,000 or 5,000 KRW on top. The base fare in Daejeon is 4,300 KRW for the first 1.8 kilometres (since July 2023), and a late-night surcharge applies from 23:00 to 04:00, which was 20% in 2025 and went up to 30% in March 2026. To catch a taxi from campus, walk to the West Gate, the campus exit by the river on the west side, or a bit further along the river.

### First-week list

On landing day, go straight to your dorm, take a quick shower and start on the forms. I let mine drag on, and they stressed me out for a whole week. Korea is technologically advanced but doesn't rely on technology for forms, so expect to write the same information by hand on several paper forms for one or two different offices, and set aside a full day for it. The first two days are stressful, and after that it's super fun.

Most errands happen in W2-1, where you find ISSS, IRT and the on-campus immigration office, and in E15. This was my list in fall 2025. The dates change every year and even moved around within KAIST's emails (dorm check-in went from 27 to 22 to 21 August), so go by the latest ISSS email and [KAIST's academic calendar](https://www.kaist.ac.kr/en/html/edu/03100101.html).

- **From 21 August:** move in and collect your student card from the dorm supervisor (see Campus life).
- **25 to 29 August:** pick up your registration packet, the bundle of forms ISSS hands out and collects again (W2-1 Multipurpose Hall, from 28 August in E15), and pay the orientation fee.
- **29 August:** orientation and the buddy ceremony in E15.

- **1 to 2 September:** hand in the packet in the W2-1 Multipurpose Hall and pay for your bedding (blanket, pillow and sheet), which goes back to the dorm supervisor when you leave.
- Get online before **13 September**, when the temporary Wi-Fi network "Welcome_KAIST" stopped working in 2025. ISSS sends a guide for the permanent one.
- Start asking around for a bike (see Campus life), because the campus is big and attendance counts toward your grade.

### Cash for the first week

ISSS told us to arrive with enough won in cash for the dorm fee, the residence card, textbooks and daily expenses, since a lot of the first payments are cash only.

| Payment | Amount (2025) | How to pay |
|---|---|---|
| Airport bus | 27,700 KRW standard | Ticket booth at the airport |
| Orientation fee | 20,000 KRW | Cash, at packet pick-up |
| Bedding rental | 20,000 KRW | Cash, at packet hand-in |
| Dorm fee and deposit | See KDS | Won only, cash or transfer at Woori Bank |
| Residence card | 35,000 KRW | KAIST's page says cash, so bring it |

The Woori Bank branch in W2 is open on weekdays from 09:00 to 16:00, with a cash machine open around the clock, and there's another in N13 (as listed in 2026). My Danish card was refused at most cash machines, so bring a backup card (see Money).

### Residence card

Anyone staying more than 90 days must register for a residence card within 90 days of arriving, and KAIST tells you to apply right after arrival. For me it was "one of the biggest messes ever", with lots of papers and forms, so prepare as much as you can in advance.

Getting the card early matters if you want to travel. If your visa is single entry, leaving Korea before the card is issued uses the visa up (see Before you fly), and many exchange students go abroad over Chuseok (the Korean harvest holiday), a week off in early October 2025. A Korean bank account, a cheap phone plan and the payment apps all want the card too.

The official route is ISSS's group application. In 2025 you handed in your documents in a hall where student volunteers helped with the forms, and went for your fingerprints later at the Global Talent Visa Center, the Korean immigration office on campus. People I knew who went this way waited about a month.

I went straight to the Global Talent Visa Center, which is in W2-1, the same building as that hall. From the hall, continue right past the café and go up the stairs to the second floor. It's open on weekdays from 09:00 to 18:00, you can apply there directly, and they give you a time to come back and collect the card. Mine took 2 to 3 weeks, so if you need to leave Korea and come back during the semester, do it my way. ISSS later emailed me about my missing group application, and telling them I'd filed directly settled it.

According to [KAIST's immigration page](https://io.kaist.ac.kr/m/campus/student_immigration.do) and the immigration service, you need these (2025).

<!-- checklist:residence-card -->
- the application form
- your passport, plus copies of the photo page and the visa page
- one passport-style photo, 3.5 × 4.5 centimetres
- an enrollment certificate from the machine in E11
- proof of residence from KDS
- the fee, 35,000 KRW since 1 January 2025 (KAIST's page still says 30,000 KRW)
- your fingerprints, which the Global Talent Visa Center takes

For the photo, there's a photographer just outside the West Gate. Go out, turn right and follow the river, and it's on the right-hand side. After a very quick session he edits the photo "so you look like a K-pop star", smoothing out every flaw and asymmetry and brightening your skin, which is humbling and also quite fun. I think that's the photo I used for my residence card.

My card came before my family trip to Rome on 14 October, and getting back into Korea afterwards was very easy (see Travel).

### The machine queue

Part of the paperwork is an automatic machine where you get your student status approved, probably the certificate machine in E11 that KAIST's pages mention for the enrollment certificate. Talk to the people around you in that queue, because it's where I met my first friends (see People).

### Phone and data

I was stubborn about not getting a Korean SIM card (the physical chip card from a phone company) and lived on eSIMs, digital SIM cards you download to your phone, which was pretty tough. I went through seven data plans, four from Nomad and then three from Airalo, kept getting low-data warnings, and found switching between the two providers confusing.

Get a proper SIM for the four months, with 30 to 50 gigabytes of data a month and calling. All my exchange friends got theirs from a stall they called "Snail Road", so ask the other exchange students where it is. It's set up every day and isn't an official Korean vendor, so it looks a bit shady, but it's cheap and gives you almost unlimited data. Official providers cost more, and reports from other KAIST students mention Chingu Mobile, which you can pick up at Incheon, and budget carriers such as KT M Mobile, at under 20,000 KRW a month for 7 gigabytes or more once you have your residence card and a Korean bank card (2023 to 2025 figures).

KakaoPay, Toss and Naver Pay, the Korean payment apps, want a residence card, a Korean phone number (they start with 010) in your name and a Korean bank account.

Before you fly, install Uber or Kakao T, KakaoTalk (the messaging app most Koreans use) and Naver Map or KakaoMap, because Google Maps is weak in Korea, and keep your Danish number active for login codes from home.
