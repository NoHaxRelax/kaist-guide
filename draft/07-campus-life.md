## Campus life

### Dorm assignment

Housing is close to guaranteed, and the process was well organised. For your first semester, ISSS applies for a dorm room on your behalf, and you only fill in a survey they email you in the summer. In 2025 it was due on 18 July and also signed you up for the buddy programme (see People).

Rooms are assigned at random, and you can't choose the room type. The assignments appear on [KDS](https://kds.kaist.ac.kr). In fall 2025 they showed up from about 18 August, and only once the campus clinic had approved your health form (see Before you fly), so get that form in early. If you stay for a second semester, you apply for a room yourself on KDS.

Most exchange students live in W6, which holds two halls, Mir Hall for men and Narae Hall for women. An exchange report from 2024 to 2025 says about 98% of exchange students lived on campus, most of them in W6. I lived in Mir Hall.

### The W6 dorm

W6 is split by gender, for safety and comfort. As you walk in, the men's side is on the left and the women's side is on the right. Residents share a gym, a study space and a convenience store in the basement. The laundry rooms are on every other floor, and the floors in between have hangout areas.

The basement store sells quick food that's fairly healthy, and it was open during my semester, but KAIST's campus services page listed it as closed in 2026, so check before you count on it. The same page lists the stores in E8, W4 and W4-3 as open from 18:00 to 02:00, and the one in N12 on weekdays from 08:00 to 02:00.

There are no kitchens in the dorms, only hot-water and drinking-water dispensers on each floor, and cooking in the dorm earns penalty points. If you really want to cook, ISSS runs the International Kitchen, a shared kitchen for international students in the Nanum building (W7). In fall 2025 it had 100 places and cost 15,000 KRW for the fall semester or 20,000 KRW for fall and winter, with sign-up opening on 25 August, and you bring your own utensils.

### Rooms and roommates

KAIST's 2025 fact sheet describes the rooms as doubles or triples, with a bed, closet, desk and chair for each person. Reports from W6 describe a mix of single and double rooms with their own shower, and mine was a double.

In 2025 first-semester exchange students could rent a blanket, pillow and bed sheet for 20,000 KRW in cash, which you hand back to the dorm supervisor before you leave. Students in earlier years found this bedding thin and bought a duvet in the first days.

You can't choose your roommate either. A 2023 exchange student wrote that KAIST tended to pair people of the same nationality, but that didn't happen to me. My roommate was two years younger than me, which I think is typical, since Korean students rarely take gap years. He was fun to live with, even if he sometimes stayed up late. He signed up for 8 or 9 courses to try to finish his degree a year early, and seeing how stressed that made him, until he dropped 2 or 3 of them, is why I tell people not to overbook themselves (see Academics).

### Dorm fees and deposit

The dorm is very cheap. I remember paying about 4,000 DKK in total for the whole semester, and the budget I made before leaving said 4,400 DKK. Prices change, so check the current fee table on [KAIST's dormitory page](https://io.kaist.ac.kr/campus/student_dormitory.do).

| What | Amount | Year and source |
|---|---|---|
| What I paid | About 4,000 DKK for the semester | Fall 2025, my memory |
| Mir/Narae double room on KAIST's fee table | About 620,000 KRW per 4 months (roughly 2,900 DKK) | Undated KAIST page, still online in 2026. Its columns are jumbled, so treat it as a rough guide |
| KAIST's official estimate | 550 to 900 US dollars per semester | 2025 fact sheet |

You pay the dorm fee and the deposit in won only, in cash or by transfer, after you collect your registration packet from ISSS in the first days (see Arrival and first weeks).

### Move-in

In fall 2025 move-in opened on 21 August, from 08:00 to 22:00. Dorm supervisors are on duty around the clock, and if you arrive outside those hours, you go through the West Dormitory Support Center in W4.

<!-- checklist:move-in -->
- Collect your student card from the dorm supervisor, as dorm residents did in 2025. The card opens your room door, and anyone arriving before 25 August needed a temporary access card from the Safety and Security Team in W8.
- Clean the room before you unpack. Exchange reports from 2022 and 2024 describe stained mattresses, some mould, clogged drains and broken chairs. Report real defects to the dorm team, and in 2024 ISSS moved one student to a new room within 4 hours.
- Photograph the room and submit the move-in checklist on KDS. In 2025 ISSS said it had to be sent from a computer, and skipping it can cost you part of your deposit.
- Buy the basics, which for most people means a duvet, cleaning supplies, detergent, hangers and hooks. A 2024 student recommends the big Daiso (a cheap household-goods chain) by Wolpyeong, the nearest subway station, 1.7 kilometres from campus, with a shared taxi back. The Homeplus supermarket in Yuseong has a smaller Daiso and is reachable by bus 5 or 104. Hanbit Plaza in Eoeun-dong has a Lotte grocery store about 7 minutes from the West Gate, the campus exit on the west side, near W6.

### Dorm rules and penalty points

The dorm rules are stricter than most Europeans are used to. Breaking them earns penalty points, and at 100 points you're expelled from the dorm for good. The points below are from the [dorm rules page](https://kds.kaist.ac.kr/dormInfo/dormRule.do) in 2026.

| Points | What for |
|---|---|
| 100 (out in one go) | Smoking in the dorm, having someone of the other gender sleep over, theft (bikes included), gambling, moving out without the move-out checklist |
| 80 | Noise between 23:00 and 08:00, disturbing your roommate |
| 50 | Entering a room of the other gender, unannounced guests, cooking, keeping alcohol, heaters, pets, parking a bike or scooter in the wrong place |
| 20 | Drying laundry in the lounge |

Kettles and hair dryers are allowed. Personal heaters are banned, so bring warm clothes for December (see Before you fly). Moving out has its own checklist, clearance form and deposit refund (see Leaving).

### The campus

The campus is huge and beautiful, with a lot of nature, old paths with cobblestone stairs, and plenty of rooftops with a view over the area. It's full of restaurants, cafés, clubs and gyms, and the sports facilities are very good.

For studying, there's the study space in W6, the library, which a 2024 report says is open until midnight, and the study area in the ACC (Academic Cultural Complex, E9), which the same report says is open around the clock.

### The W8 startup floor

My favourite spot on campus was the third floor of W8, the building of the KAIST Startup Institute. It has a hangout area with free coffee and tea, a fridge for your food, good meeting rooms and workspaces, and a terrace overlooking campus.

The floor is reserved for startup teams the institute selects each semester, though, and I got asked to leave once. If you go anyway, play it nice and don't look like you're asleep over your laptop. If you have a real startup project, ask the institute about the selection for your semester.

### Gyms and sports

I used the dorm gym and the big gym in the middle of campus, which I think is the Ryu Keun-chul Sports Complex (N3). I also played ultimate frisbee with friends on the big grass field and went running around campus.

In 2025 and 2026 KAIST listed eight free dorm gyms open around the clock, W6's among them. The paid weight room in the Sports Complex gives out places through a monthly lottery with odds of 10 to 1 up to 30 to 1, and it costs 30,000 KRW a month or 5,000 KRW for a day pass. It's open on weekdays from 07:00 to 23:00 and on Saturdays from 08:00 to 18:00. The courts, table tennis, climbing wall and jogging track are free, and students can use the pool for 1,000 KRW a visit, though the pages don't say whether exchange students count. Check [sports.kaist.ac.kr](https://sports.kaist.ac.kr) for the current rules.

### The campus sauna

KAIST has its own sauna next to the swimming pool, and there's only one pool on campus. It isn't fancy, but after a long day it's out of this world, and it costs almost nothing. You get a sauna plus hot and cold showers or baths. Gather some friends in your WhatsApp group chat and go together.

### Distances on campus

From W6, my walk to class averaged about 10 minutes, and by bike it took about 2. Kaimaru is a 5 to 10 minute walk, though a 2024 exchange report puts the far buildings at 15 to 20 minutes on foot.

One of your courses will always be far away, and attendance counts toward the grade in many courses (see Academics), which is why I tell everyone to get a bike. For trips off campus, walk to the West Gate or a bit further along the river to catch a taxi (see Arrival and first weeks for fares).

### Bikes

I got mine by shouting after a guy who was cycling past while pulling a second bike.

"Hey man, are you selling this bike?"

"Yes."

At the end of the semester, my cohort started a group to sell our bikes to the next exchange cohort. I'm not sure it's still running, but if you get to know people, someone will sell you a bike. Other ways to get one:

- ARA ([ara.kaist.ac.kr](https://ara.kaist.ac.kr)), KAIST's student bulletin board, where many exchange students have found second-hand bikes, according to a 2023 report.
- The campus bike shop behind N10, the undergraduate library. KAIST's 2026 listing has it open on weekdays from 08:30 to 18:30 and on Saturdays from 09:00 to 18:00 during the semester, with prices 10 to 30% below market. A 2019 handbook quoted 60,000 to 200,000 KRW for a new bike with 6 months of free maintenance.
- Tashu, Daejeon's public bike share. Since 2022 the first hour of every rental has been free. You unlock bikes in the Tashu app between 05:00 and midnight, and stations have been listed at N11, E8, E11, W4, the Main Gate and the West Gate. Foreigners can sign up after phone verification, but it's unclear whether that needs a Korean number.

Register your bike with KAIST. Registration has been mandatory since 2018 (you register online and collect a plate), and unregistered bikes can be confiscated and scrapped after 6 months. Get a decent lock too, since KAIST has counted about 40 to 50 bike thefts a year in the past, and park only where it's allowed, because the wrong spot at the dorm costs 50 penalty points. In December, sell your bike or pass it on to the next cohort, since abandoned bikes get cleared away (see Leaving).
